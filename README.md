# Improving trastuzumab scFv against HER2

Breast Cancer Awareness Hackathon, Zurich, 31 October 2026 (organised by HerCode, challenge by Adaptyv Bio).
Track: **Build a better cancer therapeutic. Trastuzumab scFv optimisation on HER2.**

Source of truth: `FINAL Adaptyv - Zurich hackathon deck (20261030).pdf` (in this folder).

---

## 1. The challenge in one paragraph

Trastuzumab is a main therapy for HER2-overexpressing breast cancer (20-30% of cases). It binds the HER2 extracellular domain close to the membrane (PDB 1N8Z, 2.52 Å). We are given the trastuzumab scFv (242 aa) and must propose **mutated variants that bind HER2 better than the parent (KD 0.82 nM, BLI)**. Each team's best 15 sequences are made and measured by Adaptyv in Lausanne (KD, kon, koff, raw sensorgrams, about 3 weeks later, public on ProteinBase). The track winner gets 45 slots.

## 2. Scope

### In scope
- Sequence-level optimisation of the trastuzumab scFv (point mutations in framework and/or CDRs).
- In-silico ranking of candidates with structure-based confidence metrics (ipSAE from a Boltz-2 cofold with HER2).
- A pool of **15 submitted sequences** plus a **pitch** explaining models used, positions changed and why.

### Out of scope
- De novo binder design (new miniproteins or nanobodies). We are optimising a known binder.
- Wet-lab work (Adaptyv does it), Fc/IgG formatting, ADCC or in-vivo efficacy, clinical claims.
- Changing the scFv architecture (see hard constraints).

### Hard constraints (from the deck, a violation likely means disqualification)
| Rule | Value |
|---|---|
| Mutations from parent | **at least 5** per variant |
| Format | single chain, order **VH - (GGGGS)3 - VL** |
| Length | **up to 260 aa** |
| Submission | **15 variants** per team (45 for the track winner) |

Note: the reverse order (VL-linker-VH) bound at 1.83 nM, so keep VH first as required.

### Judging criteria
1. A creative approach to design optimisation.
2. The tools used and why.
3. **ipSAE of the submitted pool.**
4. Pitch and Q&A.

### Success definition
- **Primary:** a pool that is valid, diverse, and has high ipSAE in Boltz-2 cofolds.
- **Stretch:** at least one variant with KD below 0.82 nM when the lab data arrives.
- Realistic expectation: the parent is already sub-nanomolar, so many variants will be neutral or worse. A portfolio across risk levels is the right strategy (section 5, step 7).

Numbering notes:
- VH and VL use plain sequential PDB numbering. **VH position n = scFv index n; VL position n = scFv index 135 + n** (VH 120 + linker 15).
- HER2 (chain C) is numbered 1-607; **UniProt = PDB + 22**.
- The crystal HER2 stops at UniProt 629, the deck's ectodomain goes to 652. Use one construct consistently in all cofolds.
- The structure is a Fab (separate chains, no linker). Use it for contacts and backbone-fixed design on VH/VL, and expect the linker and VH-VL orientation to come from the predictor.
- Do not mutate the variable-domain cysteines that form the intra-domain disulfides: VH C22-C96 and VL C23-C88 (PDB numbering; scFv index VH 22, 96 and VL 158, 223). The other two antibody disulfides are in the constant domains, which the scFv does not have.

### Interface with HER2 (heavy atoms within 4.5 Å)

20 antibody residues contact HER2 (epitope: HER2 PDB 557-605, i.e. UniProt 579-627, domain IV).

| Region | Contact residues (PDB number, scFv index, distance Å) |
|---|---|
| H1 | Y33 (33, 3.4) |
| H2 | R50 (50, **2.6**), Y52 (52, 4.4), Y57 (57, 3.5), T58 (58, 4.1), R59 (59, 3.4) |
| H3 | W99 (99, 4.1), G103 (103, **2.4**), F104 (104, 4.3), Y105 (105, 3.3) |
| L1 | D28 (163, 4.5), N30 (165, 3.0), T31 (166, 3.1), A32 (167, 3.7) |
| L2 | S50 (185, 3.6), F53 (188, 3.6) |
| L3 | H91 (226, **2.9**), Y92 (227, 3.0), T93 (228, 4.1), T94 (229, **2.8**) |

How we use it:
- Treat these 20 positions as **high risk**. The ones with contacts under about 3 Å (R50, G103, H91, T94, N30, T31, Y92) should stay fixed in the safe and moderate tiers.
- Look for candidate mutations in the **second shell** (residues next to the contacts, filter `min_dist_to_her2` between 4.5 and about 8 Å), in non-contacting CDR positions, and in the framework.
- Contacts are static crystal contacts. Distance alone does not say whether a mutation helps or hurts, it only says where binding is most sensitive.

| Deck tool | Role for us | Claude skill available here |
|---|---|---|
| ESM2 / ESM3 | Zero-shot single and double mutant likelihoods (free signal) | `adaptyv:esm` |

| Mosaic | Composite objective (affinity + expression + developability) | `adaptyv:mosaic` |

### Protein language models (PLMs) to use for proposing mutations

All are transformer-based. Used zero-shot: mask a position (or score the full mutant), and compare the mutant residue's log-likelihood with the parent's. No training is needed. Model names and checkpoints should be verified before use.

| Model | Type | Input | What we use it for | Caveat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **ESM2** (e.g. 650M; `adaptyv:esm`) | General protein masked LM | Single sequence (the scFv, 242 aa) | Score all 242 x 19 single mutants, then pairs among the top singles | Biased to typical residues: good for stability, weaker for affinity |
| **ESM3** (`adaptyv:esm`) | General multimodal generative LM (sequence, structure, function) | Sequence, optionally structure | Second opinion to ESM2; structure-conditioned scoring | Check licence and model size / access |
| **AbLang2** | Antibody-specific paired heavy/light masked LM | Paired VH + VL (split the scFv at the linker) | Masked scan over framework and CDRs | Germline-biased; favours common residues |
| **DSM** (Synthyra) | Masked-diffusion ESM2 | Sequence, partly masked | Sample coherent multi-mutation variants with the parent partly masked | Same bias as ESM2; sampled sequences must be re-checked against the rules |
| **ProFam-1** | Family-as-prompt PLM, open weights | Prompt set of related antibodies + query | Score mutations in the context of a trastuzumab-like family (human VH3 / VK1) | Result depends on the prompt set we choose |
| **EVOLVEpro** | PLM embeddings + few-shot active learning | Embeddings + measured labels | Only if we have labels (round 2 or ProteinBase seed data) | Not useful zero-shot |

Related structure-conditioned models (not PLMs, see steps 4 and 6): AbMPNN / ProteinMPNN (`adaptyv:proteinmpnn`), and ESM-IF as an optional extra.

Practical notes:
- For paired models (AbLang2), split the scFv into VH (residues 1-120) and VL (136-242) and exclude the linker. Map positions back to scFv numbering.
- For single-chain models (ESM2/ESM3/DSM), score the full 242 aa scFv. Optionally also score VH and VL separately to check that results are robust to the linker.
- Combine scores by rank agreement across models, not by averaging raw values (the scales differ).
- Each model writes its own `data/scores/<model>_scan.csv` with one row per (position, mutant residue). Merge them on `scfv_index` + `mut` into one `data/scores/plm_scan.csv` (one LLR column per model) so voting in step 5 is one query.

## 5. Step-by-step implementation

Suggested time boxes assume a one-day hackathon. Adjust to the actual schedule.

### Step 0. Environment (about 20 min)
1. Run the `adaptyv:setup` skill: Modal auth, biomodals directory, GPU access.
2. Create a working layout:
   ```
   Protein_Design/
     README.md
     data/        parent.fasta, 1N8Z.pdb, her2_ecd.fasta, scores/*.csv
     scripts/     validate.py, scan_*.py, cofold_*.py, rank.py
     results/     candidates.csv, final_15.csv, cofolds/
     pitch/       slides, notes
   ```
3. Smoke test one Boltz-2 run before anything else so GPU problems surface early.

### Step 1. Validator first (about 20 min)
Write `scripts/validate.py` and use it on every candidate list from now on. It checks:
- Exactly VH + `GGGGSGGGGSGGGGS` + VL composition, VH first, total length at most 260 (parent is 242).
- Hamming distance to the parent at least 5 (count substitutions in aligned positions; if indels are used, define the count conservatively).
- Only the 20 standard amino acids.
- Uniqueness within the pool.

This is the most important script: a rule violation wastes a slot.

### Step 2. Prepare the target and parent (about 30 min)
1. `adaptyv:pdb`: download 1N8Z, extract chain B (VH), chain A (VL) and the HER2 chain.
2. `adaptyv:uniprot`: fetch P04626 and cut Thr23-Thr652 for the ectodomain.
3. Verify the deck sequence matches the PDB chains (note any PDB vs. deck differences).
4. Number the parent sequence (Kabat/IMGT, e.g. with ANARCI) and write out a position table: scFv index, region (FR/CDR), contact with HER2 (distance cutoff, e.g. any heavy atom within 4.5-5 Å), buried or exposed.
5. Decide the cofold target size. Trastuzumab binds the membrane-proximal domain IV, so a truncated HER2 (domain IV plus some context) would make Boltz-2 much faster than the full 630 aa. Choose the cutoff from the 1N8Z contacts and keep it identical across all runs, otherwise ipSAE values are not comparable.

### Step 3. Baseline: score the parent (about 30 min)
1. Cofold the parent scFv + HER2 with Boltz-2 (`adaptyv:boltz`), several seeds.
2. Compute ipSAE (`adaptyv:ipsae`), report ipSAEmin, ipTM, pLDDT.
3. Sanity check: does the predicted pose match 1N8Z (RMSD of the interface, same paratope)? If not, fix the setup (templates, constraints, truncation) before scoring variants. Every later score is relative to this baseline.
4. Also cofold the reverse-order and a shuffled-linker control if time allows, to see how well ipSAE tracks the known 0.82 vs 1.83 nM difference. This is our only calibration signal.

### Step 4. Generate candidate mutations from several independent sources (about 90 min)
Run these in parallel; diversity of evidence is the creative part of the pitch.

| Source | What to do |
|---|---|
| ESM2/ESM3 (`adaptyv:esm`) | Score all 242 x 19 single mutants (about 4.6k), then plausible doubles. Keep mutations whose likelihood is at least as good as the parent residue. |
| AbLang2 | Masked scan over framework and CDRs with the paired model. |
| DSM / ProFam-1 | Independent masked-diffusion / family-prompt proposals. |
| AbMPNN / ProteinMPNN (`adaptyv:proteinmpnn`) | Redesign selected non-core, interface-adjacent positions with the backbone fixed on the Fab-HER2 complex. Keep antigen-contacting key residues fixed. |
| Mosaic (`adaptyv:mosaic`) | Composite loss: Boltz/AF2 interface confidence + stability/expression + developability. Restrict to a small set of positions. |

Guidance for what to mutate:
- Language models mostly propose "more germline-like / more common" residues. That tends to help **stability and expression** and rarely hurts binding, a good low-risk baseline.
- Structure-based design (MPNN, Mosaic) is where **affinity** gains can come from, and also where most failures come from. Limit it to the CDR periphery and second-shell positions.
- Treat CDR-H3 and the central paratope contacts as high risk.
- Do not mutate the Cys residues that form the disulfide bonds, nor the linker (it must remain `(GGGGS)3`).

#### Headline result: the sequence models are weak at affinity

**Against the structural proxy.** Across all four PLMs, `contact_sens_auc` (do CDR residues touching HER2 look *less* tolerant than CDR residues that do not?) comes out **below chance**: AbLang2 0.25, ESM-1v 0.18, ESM2-650M 0.26, ESM2-150M 0.30, against BLOSUM62 0.48 and random 0.52. They rate HER2-contacting residues as **more** mutable than the rest of the CDRs.

The cause is visible in the data: trastuzumab is a humanised mouse antibody whose paratope was engineered, so its contact residues are deliberately atypical and the models want to normalise them. The sharpest case is **VH R50**, the closest contact at 2.6 Å: AbLang2's three top-scoring mutations in the entire scan are R50V/I/L, while the ESM models score the same substitutions negatively — the most domain-specific model is the most wrong at the most important position.

**Against measured binding** (Mason et al., ~36,000 variants, scFv 99-108 — see the next section), the picture is sharper and partly different:

| Model | Stratified AUROC vs measured binding |
|---|---|
| **ESM-1v** | **0.637** |
| BLOSUM62 | 0.562 |
| ESM2-650M (split) | 0.562 |
| ESM2-150M | 0.556 |
| AbLang2 | 0.541 |
| ESM2-650M | 0.537 |
| random | 0.517 |
| *baseline: fewer mutations* | *0.500* |
| **supervised one-hot (trained on binding)** | **0.839 – 0.891** |
| supervised ESM2 embeddings (n_train=1500) | 0.852 |

Note the embedding row: ESM2 embeddings reach 0.852, but one-hot at the *same* training size reaches 0.871 (and 0.875 at just 375 examples). For 10 fixed positions, one-hot is already the complete representation, and our mean-pooling over the window discards position. Embeddings would earn their keep generalising to positions the labels never varied — which this dataset cannot test.

So "the PLMs are blind to affinity" is **too strong**. ESM-1v carries real signal (0.637, clearly above a substitution matrix). Every other model sits at or below BLOSUM62. And a one-hot logistic regression trained on binding data reaches 0.84–0.89 — scale on the naturalness objective does not substitute for data on the affinity objective.

Consequences, enforced in the pipeline:
- These models pick the **safe tier** (folding, expression, developability), not affinity.
- HER2-contact residues are hard-filtered out of every shortlist.
- Affinity gains must come from the structure stage (Boltz-2 / ipSAE, AbMPNN, Mosaic) or from measured data.
- The measured benchmark is worth putting **in the pitch** — it is a falsifiable test of our own tools.

#### Measured-affinity benchmark (Mason et al. CDR-H3)

Script: [scripts/mason_affinity.py](scripts/mason_affinity.py). Data in `data/mason/` (from `github.com/dahjan/DMS_opt`), outputs in `results/mason/`.

~36,000 yeast-display trastuzumab CDR-H3 variants sorted antigen-positive / antigen-negative. The 10 randomised residues map to **scFv 99-108** (WT `WGGDGFYAMD`), verified by locating the constant `CSR` motif. The window contains 4 of our 20 HER2 contacts and 2 consensus positions.

**The confound.** The library is combinatorial (median 8 mutations) and binder rate collapses from 0.93 at 3 mutations to 0.07 at 10. Summed LLRs track mutation count almost by construction, so all AUROCs are reported **stratified by mutation count**. Two checks confirm the stratification works: the "fewer mutations" baseline drops from 0.774 overall to exactly 0.500 stratified, and random sits at 0.517.

**Verdict on our own picks** (restricted to the 99 mutations observed 50+ times):

| Finding | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Confirmed** | D102S/T/G (84th–89th percentile, unanimous 4/4), G101S (75th) |
| **Refuted** | **A106T** — 3 votes, 39th percentile across 3,968 observations. Drop it. |
| **Lost to our own filter** | **M107F is the best measured mutation of all 190** (11,565 observations) with 4/4 votes, removed by `suspect_wt` because AbLang2 distrusted the parent Met. M107Y (93rd) lost the same way. |
| **Right position, wrong substitution** | The 10 best measured mutations are almost all at **D102** (P, Y, F, A, V, H, I) and most got **0–2 votes**. Nearly any substitution at D102 helps; our models found the position but ranked substitutions poorly. |
| **Filter worth keeping** | D102C measures well (91st) but is filtered by the no-new-cysteine rule. Keep it — a free Cys is a liability a binding assay cannot see. |

**Limits.** CDR-H3 only (says nothing about our H1/H2/framework/VL candidates). Yeast display with avidity effects and display-level confounding, not SPR/BLI. Binary bins, no K_D. Additive scoring ignores epistasis, which is a floor on model performance rather than a ceiling.

**Note on `measured_rho` in `results/eval/report.md` (Layer 1b).** That per-mutation Spearman is computed over only 99 mutations and is too noisy to rank models — `random` scores 0.224 there, above most real models. Use the variant-level AUROC above (n = 36,368) instead.

C = same, carrying the `N55S` deamidation fix (`N55-G56` motif). **D = model-only control with no measured mutations at all** — since Adaptyv measures all 15, these slots test whether measured-data-guided design actually beat model-only design.

#### How the binder rate is measured

It is an **empirical frequency from the Mason experiment, not a model output**. The chain is:

1. Each yeast cell displays one trastuzumab CDR-H3 variant on its surface.
2. The cells are mixed with fluorescently labelled HER2.
3. FACS sorts them on fluorescence into an **antigen-positive** and an **antigen-negative** gate.
4. Both populations are sequenced, so every variant carries a binary label: positive (1) or negative (0).
5. For a three-mutation core we take the library variants that carry **all three**, and compute
   `binder rate = (carriers labelled 1) / (all carriers)`.

Worked example, core `G100A+G101A+D108A`: 31 library variants carry all three, 27 are labelled binder, so the rate is 27/31 = **0.871**.

**The baseline that matters.** The library-wide binder rate is 0.290, but comparing against it would flatter these cores. Carriers hold *other* random mutations too — median **7** in the 10-residue window — and binder rate falls steeply with mutation count. So each core is compared against a **mutation-count-matched** baseline: the mean library binder rate at each carrier's own mutation count. That gives 0.329 for the example above, hence **2.65x enrichment**.

**What the number is not.**
- **Not a K_D**, and not a prediction for our 5-mutation variant. It is the measured rate for library variants containing the core *plus other mutations*.
- **"Binder" means binds HER2**, not *beats the 0.82 nM parent*. The parent is already optimised, so a measured binder is not automatically an improvement.
- **Yeast display, not SPR/BLI.** Multivalent display creates avidity effects, and a poorly displayed variant reads as a non-binder regardless of intrinsic affinity.
- **Only the H3 mutations are covered.** The non-H3 changes (N28T, A79V, T54G, I29F, A49G, V168L, N55S, A169S, I241V) have **no affinity evidence at all** — they carry model votes only.

#### Why no variant carries more than three H3 mutations

The first version of this pool stacked 5-6 measured-good H3 mutations ranked by the *sum* of their single-mutation effects. Checking that against the library showed **zero variants carried those combinations** (the one case with any data was a non-binder), so the sums were extrapolation across untested epistasis. Three-mutation cores have 30-94 real observations each, so the pool is now selected on measured **combination** binder rate instead. This is the single biggest design change the measured data forced.

#### Known gap: ipSAE is not computed

The deck judges **the ipSAE of the pool**. That needs Boltz-2 on a GPU, which has not been run. This pool is optimised on measured binding plus model consensus, *not* on the judged metric. Running `adaptyv:boltz` + `adaptyv:ipsae` over these 15 is the highest-value remaining step.

#### Method 1 detail: ESM2 zero-shot single-mutant scan

Script: [scripts/esm2_scan.py](scripts/esm2_scan.py) (full documentation is in its header). Runs locally (PyTorch, Apple MPS / CUDA / CPU), no Modal needed. The same script runs any standard-architecture ESM checkpoint via `--model`, which is how ESM2-150M and ESM-1v were scored.

**What it does.** For each of the 242 positions and each of the 19 alternative residues (4,598 single mutants) it computes the log-likelihood ratio `LLR = log p(mutant) - log p(parent residue)` from ESM2 (`facebook/esm2_t33_650M_UR50D`). Two standard modes are computed (Meier et al. 2021): *masked marginals* (mask the position, 242 forward passes, primary score `llr_masked`) and *wild-type marginals* (one pass, `llr_wt`, cross-check). `--context split` scores VH and VL separately without the linker as a robustness check.

**Run.**
```
python3 -I scripts/esm2_scan.py --model facebook/esm2_t12_35M_UR50D   # 30 s smoke test
python3 -I scripts/esm2_scan.py --model facebook/esm2_t33_650M_UR50D  # main run, about 3 min on an M-series Mac
python3 -I scripts/esm2_scan.py --context split                        # robustness check
```

**Outputs** (`data/scores/`):
| File | Content |
|---|---|
| `esm2_t33_650M_scan.csv` | All 4,598 mutants with region, distance to HER2, `llr_masked`, `llr_wt`, rank |
| `esm2_t33_650M_positions.csv` | Per position: probability of the parent residue, entropy, mean LLR, best mutation |
| `esm2_t33_650M_shortlist.csv` | First-pass filtered mutations (165 rows): not in the linker, not a disulfide Cys, not to Cys, LLR >= 0, more than 4.5 A from HER2, parent residue not "suspect" |
| `*_split_*.csv` | Same, VH and VL scored separately |

**Built-in sanity checks and what they showed (650M, scFv context).**
- Disulfide cysteines: mean LLR -9.9 for all substitutions. Correct, indexing is right.
- Masked vs. wt-marginal Spearman 0.98; scFv vs. split context position-level Spearman 0.98. The result does not depend on the linker.
- Only 2.6% of framework mutations have LLR >= 0, against 10-29% in CDRs. The model sees the framework as conserved and the CDRs as variable, as expected.
- HER2-contact residues have *higher* mean LLR (-1.1) than non-contact residues (-5.6). ESM2 therefore does **not** know which CDR residues are important for HER2 binding. It only knows what is typical for an antibody. This is the main reason it must not be used alone.

**A model artefact we found and handled.** VL F83 (scFv 218) got LLR +9.9 for F->E and positive scores for almost every substitution, which flooded the top of the ranking. Inspecting the model: the parent Phe gets p = 0.0001 while Glu gets p = 0.74, for a conserved framework residue. It reproduces in split context, so it is a model quirk rather than a linker or indexing bug (we could not find the cause). The script now flags positions where the parent residue has p < 0.01 (`suspect_wt`) and removes them from the shortlist: scFv 218 (VL F83), 32 (VH T32), 72 (VH A72), 188 (VL F188 = VL F53, a HER2 contact). Treat these as "needs a second model", not as discoveries.

**How to read the result and use it in step 5.**
- The shortlist is one vote, not a design. Highest-scoring shortlist mutations include VL R66G, VL V33L, VH S97A, VL K42E/Q, VH E1Q and VH K30S/G/T/A. Many are framework or N-terminal changes that probably help stability or expression and are unlikely to change KD.
- Interesting: VH N55S / N55G sit in the `N55-G56` motif (`PTNGYT`), a known deamidation hotspot, and are 5.3 A from HER2. Candidate for a liability fix, but a close-to-interface change that needs the structure check.
- Next: require agreement with the other models (done for AbLang2 / ESM-1v / ESM2-150M — see the consensus above) and AbMPNN, then combine into 5+ mutation variants (step 6).

**Limitations.** Single mutants only (no epistasis, pairs are the next extension). The model scores plausibility, not affinity. The score is relative to this exact scFv context.

#### Evaluation pipeline (compare every model through the same checks)

Scripts: [scripts/evaluate.py](scripts/evaluate.py) (pipeline), [scripts/validate.py](scripts/validate.py) (hackathon rule validator and mutation parser), [scripts/baselines.py](scripts/baselines.py) (BLOSUM62 and random reference "models"). Output in `results/eval/` (`report.md`, `eval_summary.png`, csv tables).

**What it can and cannot measure.** We have no measured affinities for variants until the lab results arrive (about 3 weeks after the event), so real predictive performance cannot be measured today. The pipeline has three layers:

| Layer | Needs | What it tells us |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Intrinsic sanity checks | nothing | Does the model respect known biology? Disulfide Cys positions among the most intolerant quarter (bug detector), parent-residue recovery, whether it rates HER2-contact CDR residues as less tolerant than non-contact ones, suspect-wt positions |
| 2. Cross-model agreement | 2+ models | Spearman between models (mutation and position level), top-100 overlap, a vote table of mutations ranked in the top 20% by several independent models |
| 3. Variant pool / performance | a variants CSV (`id`, `sequence` or `mutations`, optional `kd_nM`) | Rule validation, additive per-model score of each variant, pool diversity; with `kd_nM`: Spearman vs. -log10 KD and AUROC for "better than parent", against trivial predictors ("fewer mutations is better", BLOSUM62, random) |

**Adding a model.** Write `data/scores/<name>_scan.csv` with `scfv_index`, `mut` and either `score` or `llr_masked` (higher = more favourable). The next run picks it up. Names ending in `_split` and the baselines do not count as votes.

```
python3 -I scripts/baselines.py                                         # once
python3 -I scripts/evaluate.py                                          # layers 1-2
python3 -I scripts/evaluate.py --variants results/final_15.csv          # + rules and pool scores
python3 -I scripts/evaluate.py --variants labelled.csv --parent-kd 0.82 # + performance (after lab data)
```
Any labelled set works for layer 3 (e.g. a public trastuzumab mutational scan, with `--no-rules`), which is the way to get a real performance number *before* the lab data, if such a dataset is found.

**Testing and caveats.**
- Layer 3 was tested with synthetic variants and synthetic KD, kept outside the project (scratchpad). The validator caught under-5-mutation and disulfide-Cys variants. The synthetic labels were derived from ESM2 scores, so the Spearman it produced is circular and says nothing about real performance.
- A first version of the Cys check (fraction of all mutants below the mean Cys mutant) wrongly failed ESM2, because ESM2 has many other highly intolerant positions. It was replaced by the position-level percentile above. The 0.25 threshold was fixed before seeing the new values.
- Additive variant scores ignore epistasis. With only 15 labelled variants, Spearman will be very uncertain: compare models by the gap to the baselines, not by absolute value.

Output: one tidy table `results/candidates.csv` with columns: variant id, mutations (e.g. `H:S30T`), source tool(s), per-model scores.

### Step 5. Consensus filter (about 30 min)
- Rank mutations by how many independent sources support them (ESM and AbLang2 and structure design agreeing is a strong signal).
- Drop mutations with liabilities using `adaptyv:protein-qc`: new N-glycosylation motifs (N-X-S/T), unpaired Cys, extra deamidation/isomerisation hotspots (NG, DG), large hydrophobic patches, unwanted pI shifts.
- Drop mutations that break the interface in a quick local check (loss of contacts, clashes).

### Step 6. Combine into variants and cofold at scale (about 60-90 min)
1. Build several hundred to a few thousand combinations of at least 5 mutations each, starting from the consensus-ranked list.
2. Cofold with Boltz-2 (seeds fixed, same HER2 construct as the baseline), compute ipSAE with `adaptyv:ipsae`.
3. Keep only variants where ipSAEmin is at or above the parent baseline (and pLDDT is not degraded).
4. Validate every variant with `scripts/validate.py`.
5. Use `adaptyv:campaign-manager` to review the pass rate per stage. If almost nothing passes, loosen thresholds or reduce mutation count per variant, do not add more mutations.

### Step 7. Select the final 15: build a portfolio (about 30 min)
With only 15 slots and a strong parent, diversify by risk instead of picking the top 15 by one score. Suggested split (adjust once we see the data):

| Tier | Slots | Idea |
|---|---|---|
| A. Safe | about 5 | Exactly 5-6 mutations, mostly framework / stability / germline-like, high ESM + AbLang2 agreement. Goal: retain about 1 nM binding. |
| B. Moderate | about 6 | Mix of LM-supported framework changes and 1-3 CDR-periphery affinity candidates from structure design. |
| C. Bold | about 4 | CDR-focused, Mosaic / MPNN-driven, the best shot at KD below 0.82 nM. |

Selection rules: diversity (no two variants share more than about 60-70% of their mutations), every variant passes validation and the QC filters, and the pool-level mean ipSAE is high (the judging metric). Save as `results/final_15.csv`.

### Step 8. Optional: use the small-budget active-learning idea (only if there is data)
EVOLVEpro-style few-shot learning needs measured labels. We only get lab data about 3 weeks after the event, so it is mostly a **future round** (use results on ProteinBase to retrain and design round 2). Check ProteinBase for existing trastuzumab / HER2 measurements first (`proteinbase.com`), they could serve as seed labels.

## 6. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Rule violation (mutation count, length, order) | `validate.py` gate on everything, run it before submitting |
| ipSAE improves but real binding does not (cofold models are imperfect) | Calibrate with the parent and the reverse-order control; use safe-tier variants; do not rely on one predictor (optionally cross-check the top candidates with Chai or Protenix) |
| Improved scores are artefacts of a different HER2 construct | Same construct, seeds and settings for all runs |
| Too many CDR mutations destroy a sub-nM binder | Portfolio approach, keep high-risk variants to a few slots |
| Poor expression / aggregation of the scFv | LM-supported framework changes, `adaptyv:protein-qc` developability checks, avoid new hydrophobic patches |
| Running out of GPU or time | Truncated HER2 construct, small seed counts first, cache results to CSV |
